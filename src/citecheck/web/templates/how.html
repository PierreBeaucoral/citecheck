How citecheck works

Six pipeline stages, four independent checks, four open public APIs, one optional LLM call. Everything runs in the same place; nothing is sent to a third-party "AI" service for inspection beyond what you can see below.

The pipeline

Each stage produces a typed data object that the next stage consumes. Stages 3–6 run in parallel against the same resolved reference; each is independently auditable.

Extraction

Your PDF is sent to a local instance of GROBID — an open-source machine-learning model for parsing scholarly documents. We disable Crossref consolidation; we want our own DOI lookups to use the polite-pool conventions described below.

GROBID

Resolution

Each parsed reference is mapped to a canonical DOI through a three-step dispatch: Crossref by-DOI first, Crossref by-metadata second, OpenAlex as fallback. Both APIs are free; we honour their polite-pool headers so this never costs them a paid lookup.

Crossref OpenAlex

Retraction check

Two-source merge. Crossref's update-to field carries the structured retraction notice when the publisher has deposited one — but only 40% of retractions in our eval have that signal populated. OpenAlex's is_retracted flag, fed from the Retraction Watch dataset, catches the other 60%. A single-source check would miss most retractions; the merge is the empirical justification.

Crossref update-to OpenAlex is_retracted

Fabrication detection

Five signals, rule-based aggregator. Three are existence checks (does this field appear in a database?) and two are coherence checks (do two extracted fields agree?). Our published evaluation shows the coherence checks discriminate fabricated from real citations roughly twelvefold more sharply than the existence checks — the design hypothesis the paper confirms empirically.

OpenAlex /authors OpenAlex /sources OpenAlex /works

Journal quality

Eight signals scored continuously: DOAJ membership, Scopus indexing, h-index, citations per article, hand-curated concern list match, journal-flood pattern (low h-index + high works count), high APC without DOAJ listing, and publisher portfolio size. A single positive signal (DOAJ-listed) is enough to demote a venue to LOW; a single concern signal carries a venue to HIGH on its own.

DOAJ OpenAlex /sources

Claim verification (optional)

Only runs if you tick the checkbox at upload time. We fetch the cited paper's full text (Unpaywall for DOI-keyed sources, NCBI's E-utilities for PMC-only sources), chunk it at ~500 tokens, embed the chunks with a small open-weight transformer, retrieve the five most semantically similar passages to your in-text claim, and ask an open-weight LLM whether they support the claim. The LLM is prompted to enumerate qualifier and quantifier mismatches before setting the verdict; a parser guard rail forces the verdict to NOT_SUPPORTED whenever any discrepancy is found.

What citecheck cannot do

Citecheck is a screening tool, not a substitute for editorial review. Honest limitations:

  • Books and grey literature. The fabrication detector's strongest signal (author–title coherence) is by-design disabled for references without a journal field. Book recall on our 770-reference eval is 0.250 — citecheck catches one in four fabricated book references.
  • Non-English venues and pre-2010 obscure journals. Crossref and OpenAlex indexing is patchy here. A real citation to a 1971 paper in a regional journal can look identical to a fabrication to a database-only detector.
  • Misattribution to real, similar papers. If a citation references a real paper that happens to discuss the same topic but does not support the specific claim, citecheck's metadata checks all pass; only Phase 5 (claim verification) catches this — and Phase 5 only runs if you opt in.
  • Subtle scientific errors inside a correctly-cited paper. If the cited paper itself is wrong, citecheck has nothing to say about that. We check whether the citation accurately represents what the cited paper claims — not whether the cited paper's claims are true.
  • Predatory venue judgements are heuristic. Our journal-quality risk score combines reputation and concern signals, but the concern list is hand-maintained and the broader literature on predatory publishing is contested. Treat HIGH risk as "review carefully," not as a final verdict.

Open and reproducible

Citecheck is Apache-2.0 licensed. Every eval number in the companion paper has a one-line reproduction recipe. The benchmark datasets — 60 retraction DOIs, 770 fabrication references, 75 predatory-venue calibration entries, 100 hand-curated claim-verification triples — are public under permissive licences. The repository at github.com/pierrebeaucoral/citecheck has the full source, test suite, and Docker setup for self-hosting.

Reports generated through this web app are public-by-URL. The job IDs are unguessable UUIDs (128 bits) so there's no listing or directory; only people you share the URL with can find a given report. We don't keep accounts, don't track visitors, and store no PII.
